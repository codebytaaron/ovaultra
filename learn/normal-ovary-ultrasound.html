What does a normal ovary look like on ultrasound?

A short, plain-English guide to reading a normal ovary on an ultrasound image.

From Cathra · Updated November 25, 2026 · General education, not medical advice

Short answer

On ultrasound, a normal ovary in an adult of reproductive age usually looks like a small, oval, mid-gray structure with a few small, dark, round spots. Those spots are follicles, tiny fluid-filled sacs that hold developing eggs. Their number and size change throughout the menstrual cycle.

Reading the gray, black, and white

Ultrasound images are built from sound echoes, so they show structures in shades of gray:

  • Black: fluid, such as the inside of a follicle or a cyst. Fluid lets sound pass through without echoing.
  • Gray: soft tissue, such as the ovary’s inner tissue (the stroma).
  • Bright white: denser surfaces that reflect a lot of sound.

What a typical ovary shows

  • Shape and size: an oval structure, usually a few centimeters long. An ovarian volume of 10 mL or more is one of the signs doctors look for when considering polycystic ovarian morphology.
  • Small follicles: several small, dark, round follicles, usually spread through the ovary.
  • One larger follicle mid-cycle: as ovulation approaches, one follicle usually grows larger than the rest before it releases an egg.
  • After ovulation: the follicle that released the egg becomes the corpus luteum, which can look like a small, thick-walled structure.

What changes the picture

  • Cycle timing: the same ovary can look quite different at the start, middle, and end of a cycle.
  • Age: after menopause, ovaries are usually smaller and show few or no follicles. Before puberty they are also small.
  • Hormonal birth control can reduce follicle growth.
  • Simple, fluid-filled cysts are common and often part of the normal cycle. A doctor decides whether any follow-up is needed.

How the scan is done

Ovaries are usually imaged with a transvaginal ultrasound, which gives the clearest view, or a transabdominal ultrasound through the lower belly. The exam is painless or mildly uncomfortable and uses no radiation.
