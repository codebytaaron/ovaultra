Can AI detect PCOS from an ultrasound?

An honest look at what ultrasound AI can and cannot tell you about PCOS.

From Cathra · Updated November 25, 2026 · General education, not medical advice

Short answer

No. An AI tool cannot diagnose PCOS from an ultrasound alone. PCOS is diagnosed by a clinician using symptoms, blood tests, and ruling out other conditions. At most, ultrasound AI can estimate whether an image resembles a polycystic ovarian appearance, which is only one part of one diagnostic criterion.

Why an ultrasound alone isn’t enough

In adults, PCOS is diagnosed when at least two of three features are present: irregular periods or ovulation problems, signs of high androgens, and polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound (or a high AMH blood level). Other conditions must be ruled out first. An image can only speak to one of those three, and many people with polycystic-looking ovaries don’t have PCOS.

What ultrasound AI can do in research

  • Compare an image to patterns learned from labeled examples.
  • Help researchers and students study how image AI behaves, including its mistakes.
  • Highlight how dataset quality, such as duplicate or mislabeled images, affects results.

What it can’t do

  • Diagnose PCOS or any other condition.
  • Account for symptoms, hormone levels, age, or cycle timing.
  • Replace an ultrasound exam read by a qualified professional.

Be careful with PCOS AI claims

If an app says it can “detect” or “diagnose” PCOS from a photo or scan, ask how it was tested, on whose images, and whether it has been cleared for medical use. Treat high accuracy claims with caution unless they are backed by testing on images from many different sources.

Where Cathra fits

Cathra compares an ovarian ultrasound to normal and abnormal appearance patterns. Most abnormal images it learned from show a polycystic appearance, but Cathra does not detect or diagnose PCOS. It is a free research and education tool, and it runs privately in your browser.

Quick answers

Can AI diagnose PCOS?

No. PCOS is diagnosed by a clinician using symptoms, blood tests, and ultrasound or AMH, after ruling out other causes. AI cannot make that diagnosis from an image.

Does Cathra detect PCOS?

No. Cathra compares an ovarian ultrasound to normal and abnormal appearance patterns for research and education. It does not detect or diagnose PCOS or any other condition.

Do polycystic ovaries mean PCOS?

Not necessarily. Polycystic-looking ovaries are common, especially in teenagers, and are only one of three criteria used to diagnose PCOS in adults.
