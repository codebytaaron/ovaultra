Paper

Cathra: An In-Browser Classifier for Normal and Abnormal Ovarian Ultrasound Appearance

Teddy Aaron · Preprint · Zenodo, November 2026 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24118082

85.6%accuracy on 1,449 held-out test images
81.1%balanced accuracy across both classes
1,734training images, equal normal and abnormal
Abstract

I built Cathra, a free web tool that compares an existing ovarian ultrasound image to two learned patterns, normal and abnormal, and returns a similarity score for each. The model runs entirely in the browser, so images are never uploaded. I trained the current model (v2) on 1,734 images from the public PCOSGen dataset, balanced between the two classes, and tested it on 1,449 separate images. v2 reached 85.6% accuracy and 81.1% balanced accuracy, matching 91.4% of abnormal and 70.8% of normal test images. My first model (v1) reached 74.2% accuracy on the same images but recognized only 19.2% of normal ones. Cathra is for research and education only and is not a diagnostic tool.

Dataset Check

Many public medical image datasets contain the same image more than once, sometimes with different labels or in both the training and test sets. That can make a model look more accurate than it is. Cathra Dataset Check is a free tool that finds these copies in any folder of images, privately in your browser.

Teach with Cathra

A 30-minute lesson on what AI can and can’t do with medical images.

  1. Learn (5 min). Read how Cathra works together.
  2. Try (15 min). Analyze a few images and note the scores and any “Unable to assess” results.
  3. Discuss (10 min). Pick two questions below.

Where to find images: use public, de-identified research datasets, such as PCOS ultrasound datasets on Kaggle or PCOSGen. Check each dataset’s license before using it. Don’t use personal or patient scans.

Discussion questions

  • Why is a similarity score not the same as a diagnosis?
  • Version 1 scored 74% overall but got only 19% of normal images right. How can one number be misleading?
  • Why might results differ on scans from another hospital?
  • Who is responsible when a medical AI tool is wrong?
