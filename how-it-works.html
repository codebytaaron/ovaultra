How Cathra works

What it was trained on, what its scores mean, and where it falls short.

What it does

You give Cathra an existing ovarian ultrasound image. It compares the image to two visual patterns it learned during training, normal ovarian appearance and abnormal ovarian appearance, and returns a similarity score for each. It does not diagnose anything.

Training data

The model was trained on a labeled set of ovarian ultrasound images, sorted into two categories:

  • Normal: ovaries with a typical appearance.
  • Abnormal: a broad category that includes non-cancerous findings such as polycystic ovarian appearance, simple cysts, and complex cysts. An abnormal-pattern result does not mean cancer.

Dataset credit

Training images come from the PCOSGen dataset (train and test sets), created for the Auto-PCOS classification challenge by Palak Handa, Anushka Saini, Siddhant Dutta, Harsh Pathak, Nishi Choudhary, Nidhi Goel, and Jasdeep Kaur Dhanao. Published on Zenodo, 2025: train · test. Used under the CC BY 4.0 license. Images were sorted into Normal and Abnormal using the dataset’s own labels.

Reading the results

  • Each percentage is a visual similarity score: how closely the image resembles that pattern. It is not the probability that a person has any condition.
  • The higher of the two scores is shown as the result label.
  • If the higher score is below 60%, or the two scores are within 15 points of each other, Cathra shows “Unable to assess confidently” instead of picking one.

Limitations

  • The training set is small and has not been clinically validated.
  • Results on images from other hospitals, ultrasound machines, or patient populations are unknown.
  • Image quality, cropping, on-screen labels, and annotations can change the scores.
  • It only knows two broad categories. It cannot identify specific conditions or assign clinical categories such as O-RADS.
  • It will still return scores for images that are not ovarian ultrasounds.

Intended use

Research and education only. Cathra is not for screening, diagnosis, triage, or any medical decision, and it is not intended for patients. Ultrasound images must be reviewed by a qualified medical professional.
